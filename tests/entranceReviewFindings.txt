D0 ENTRY-REGION INVESTIGATION — FINDINGS, 2026-11-07

The sedentary cluster is consistent with a combination of longer, slower entry
behavior and a speed-sensitive detector, with a mixture of visible paw, tail and
head movements. The evidence does not support treating every detection as a
hind-paw slip, nor dismissing the entire cluster as artifact. Low-speed detector
behavior is important across groups, not unique to sedentary mice.

TMCS3_S07 contributes 20 of the 55 detections. Its trial 4 contributes 13 detections,
6.056 seconds in the bin and 4.850 seconds classified as stopped. Excluding this
mouse leaves 35 detections / 21.50 seconds = 1.63 per second. Thus that mouse is
influential, but it does not account for the whole descriptive difference.

2. Within speed bands, sedentary frames are not consistently more likely to
cross threshold. Exposure composition is an important explanation to consider.

For speed (5,20] px/s, frame-exceedance fractions are 34.5%, 45.4%, 47.2% for
sedentary/walk/run. At (20,40], they are 28.3%, 36.1%, 37.6%. At (0,5], they are
37.5%, 59.6%, 25.9%; the run bin contains only 54 frames from five mice.

Across all entry frames, >2.5 exceedance fractions are 20.0%, 15.1%, 11.5%.
Reweighting each group's speed-specific fractions to the same pooled six-bin speed
distribution gives 16.1%, 17.7%, 15.1%. This removes the sedentary ordering in this
particular descriptive frame calculation. It is NOT an adjusted slip count, a
causal estimate, or proof that biology is identical. Coarse speed bins, different
movement types, temporal dependence, and trial-specific baselines remain.
The reweighting and its bin weights are saved explicitly.

3. Intermediate traces demonstrate two interacting detector effects.

All 21 instrumented trial captures exactly match the original saved final traces,
speeds, centroids, frame mappings and event intervals. RawUnweighted and RawWeighted
are measured before speed adjustment; both come from the existing enhanced video,
not unprocessed camera pixels. RawWeighted already includes row and column weights.

The speed multiplier is 160/log1p(speed): about 230.8 at 1 px/s and 43.1 at 40 px/s.
Identical weighted raw motion therefore receives 5.36 times the instantaneous
amplification at 1 px/s, before smoothing. The final score additionally depends
on each traversal's finite non-stopping baseline median and MAD.

In the purposeful reviewed sample, pooled median weighted raw motion is 0.609,
0.727, 0.853 for sedentary/walk/run; median speed is 22.8, 56.9, 68.8 px/s.
Removing TMCS3_S07 from that sample changes the sedentary raw median to 0.879
and speed median to 44.2. Consequently, the low pooled sedentary raw motion is
strongly influenced by that long visit; other sedentary mice need separate review.
The reviewed sedentary trials do not have a uniformly more permissive baseline:
median trial MAD scale is 11.33 versus 9.66 (walk) and 9.55 (run). Median
pre-normalization thresholds are 59.77, 51.86, 47.53 respectively. These selected
trial summaries are not population estimates and are not weighted equally by mouse.

Examples beyond TMCS3_S07 (full filenames and intervals in review_observations.csv):

- BLIND_07 versus BLIND_11, TMCS2 walk S31 versus sedentary S15: similar raw peaks
  2.154 versus 1.854; median speeds 24.10 versus 13.50; final peaks 3.38 versus
  4.54. The lower-speed event has a higher normalized peak despite smaller raw
  motion, with a somewhat smaller baseline scale as well.
- BLIND_17 versus BLIND_23, TMCS2 run S17 versus walk S07: raw peaks 13.357 versus
  12.826; median speeds 71.65 versus 4.67; final peaks 51.75 versus 109.23.
  Strong amplification is plainly not restricted to the sedentary group. The
  walk trial also has the smaller baseline scale. Both sequences include tail
  motion; these are similar raw peaks, not identical biological movements.
- BLIND_02 versus BLIND_06, TMCS2 sedentary S28 versus run S17: raw peaks 3.847
  versus 4.190; speeds 99.12 versus 138.98; final peaks 4.03 versus 8.05. Here the
  faster run event has the higher score; duration/waveform and the smaller run

- BLIND_01 versus BLIND_05, TMCS3 sedentary S13 versus walk S04: raw peaks 4.404
  versus 3.960; speeds 46.89 versus 41.73; final peaks 9.76 versus 12.12.
  This provides a second-cohort comparison with similar amplitude and speed but
  different movement content and timing.

4. Exactly zero speed has a separate numerical failure mode.

In target trial 4, detections 11 and 12 span original frames 1728–1755 and
1778–1848. The source sequence shows the head lowered below the beam through
the intervening period. Actual weighted raw motion during frames 1756–1777 remains
positive (1.303–4.385), but all 22 pre-clamp normalized samples are nonfinite and
the final trace is zero. Speed is exactly zero at 1760–1772. The code's division
by log1p(0), smoothing and NaN-to-zero step create this artificial interruption.
A separate synthetic constant-raw check reproduces the exact gap boundaries.
Thus these two detections are not evidence of two separate hind-paw slip cycles.

The numerical issue is not sedentary-specific. Nonfinite pre-clamp entry samples
occur in 4.8%, 11.3%, 1.9% of frames in the reviewed sedentary/walk/run samples.
All exact-zero-speed entry frames in the full D0 dataset have a final score of
zero. Such zeros must not be interpreted as proof that no physical movement
occurred. Existing measures were preserved; no thresholds or code were repaired.

5. The visual review found mixed movement sources, including plausible slips.

All 13 target trial-4 detections were inspected in chronological source-frame
sequences. Most early detections accompany subtle crouched head/paw adjustments;
distinct hind-paw slip cycles cannot be established. Detections 11–12 visibly
include head tissue entering the detector band (original rows 224–240). Tail
movement coexists in later parts. Proximity to entry is not proof of contact with
the entrance platform, which is outside the camera field.

Other sedentary examples include apparent hind-paw excursions:
TMCS2_S15_T3_C08_BB_D00_20251022_THT_TRIAL3_gray.mp4, frames 1056–1099 (BLIND_21),
and TMCS2_S28_T1_C10_BB_D00_20251022_THT_TRIAL2_gray.mp4, 1363–1373 (BLIND_12).
Comparable apparent excursions occur in walk S04 (BLIND_05) and run S10 (BLIND_04).
Run S02, frames 1057–1123 (BLIND_20), shows substantial body rotation/balance
disturbance, rather than just a tail sweep. Other sequences show prominent tail
crossings (e.g. BLIND_09, 10, 15, 17, 23) or front-paw movements (BLIND_13).
These labels describe what is visible; they do not prove which pixel source
dominates each trace, and subtle/occluded movements remain uncertain.

The 37 event reviews and 10 non-event reviews are purposefully selected, not a
group-level false-positive-rate estimate. Four added controls had center speed
(0,5] px/s; no selected run trial met the same strict separation rule. Group labels
were hidden for the initial non-target sequence review, but this was not an
independent blinded expert study. Every case retains uncertainty, full filename,
original-frame interval, short full-field clip, sequence and saved trace context.

6. No obvious sedentary-specific recording difference was identified.

All 124 eligible D0 videos are 1280x356. Detected bar tops differ by cohort
(TMCS2: 207–208; TMCS3: 213–214), with overlapping thicknesses across groups.
Median background brightness is tightly similar (approximately 188.2–188.4 on
the decoded 0–255 grayscale scale across cohort/group cells). Full-field examples
show similar composition. These checks do not exclude subtle local shadows,
temporal flicker or posture-dependent masking. No gross centroid discontinuity
was apparent in reviewed intervals; the analysis crop/masks were not independently
segmented as a new ground truth. A handler's fingers are visible in a walk
non-event control, so handling exposure is not confined to sedentary detections.

Interpretation and limits

Longer low-speed residence gives more opportunities for repeated under-bar
movements and gives small positive-speed movements stronger detector gain.
Trial-specific normalization and zero-speed invalid-value handling further alter
threshold crossings and event splitting. These are supported mechanisms, but
their exact contribution to the group event-count difference is not identified
causally here. Similar raw peaks do not guarantee similar paw kinematics.

The next confirmatory step would be a prespecified review sampled by mouse/cage,
speed and raw-motion strata, with an agreed biological slip definition and a
separate numerical sensitivity analysis. Do not simply discard stopped frames
or tune thresholds by group based on this post-hoc region.
